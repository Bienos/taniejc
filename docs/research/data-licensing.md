# Data licensing and reuse

This is an engineering summary, not legal advice. Recheck current terms and get written commercial permission where terms are unclear before launch.

## Open data

- **Open Food Facts database**: ODbL; individual database contents use DbCL. Product images use CC BY-SA and may include third-party rights. Attribute Open Food Facts, respect share-alike obligations for adapted databases, and review its terms/attribution guidance. The API asks users to identify their app with a custom User-Agent and requests filling its API usage form.
- **Open Prices**: data is under ODbL. The project asks reusers to attribute the source, avoid combining data that cannot legally be released as open data, and contribute back products added through its SDK. Keep source attribution and source identifiers with observations. Any derived database containing ODbL material must be assessed for share-alike obligations before combining with proprietary retailer feeds.
- **OpenStreetMap**: ODbL attribution and database obligations apply; public geocoding/routing services have separate acceptable-use and rate policies. Do not use the public Nominatim endpoint as a high-volume production service.

## Commercial retailer and aggregator data

No reuse right is inferred from public visibility, robots behavior, newsletter availability, a consumer app, or presence in search-engine results. Do not crawl behind login, bypass controls, query private app endpoints, or republish flyer images. Obtain a license/affiliate agreement covering commercial use, images, campaign terms, store coverage, history, caching, and attribution. Until signed, link to the provider instead of importing its data.

## Images and alcohol

Display catalog images only under their applicable license and link to the source. Alcohol and other regulated products require a separate Polish advertising/legal review; the current product does not show alcohol offers or promote alcohol campaigns.
